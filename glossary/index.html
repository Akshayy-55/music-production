Glossary

70 terms. Jargon defined once so lessons stay readable.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTVW

A

Arrangement
How sections are ordered over time: intro, verse, build, drop, outro — storytelling with energy.
Audio clip
Recorded or imported sound (WAV/MP3) sitting on a timeline or in a sampler.

B

Backbeat
Emphasis on beats 2 and 4 — usually snare or clap.
Bar
A group of beats — usually 4 in dance music. Also called a measure.
Bass
Low frequencies (roughly under ~250 Hz). Kick and bassline live here; two bass sounds often clash.
Beat
One pulse in the music. In 4/4 time, you usually count four beats per bar.
Beatmatching
Making two tracks play at the same tempo and with beats aligned so they stay together.
Booth
The DJ's workspace — mixer, decks, and usually a booth monitor speaker that is not the main PA.
BPM
Beats Per Minute — how fast the pulse of a track is. House is often ~120–128 BPM; hip-hop can sit around 85–100.
Break
A section where drums or energy pull back — space for a transition or rebuild.

C

Channel EQ
The HI / MID / LOW knobs on a mixer channel. Cut clashing bass or harsh highs on one deck without changing the other.
Channel fader
The vertical slider that sets how loud that channel is in the mix, after gain and EQ. Ride it during transitions.
Clipping
Distortion when the signal is too loud for the digital ceiling — red meters mean turn down.
Closed-back headphones
Headphones that isolate sound — useful for DJ cueing and late-night practice without leaking.
Controller
Hardware with jog wheels, faders, and pads that talks to DJ software.
Crate
A playlist or folder of tracks prepared for a set — your starter library.
Crossfader
A slider that blends output from deck A to deck B. Move it gradually during transitions.
Crossfader curve
How fast the blend happens as you move the crossfader. Sharp curves cut quickly (scratching); smooth curves fade slowly (blends).
Cue
A marked point on a track (often the intro or drop) so you can jump there quickly on a DJ deck.
Cue mix
What you hear in headphones while the crowd hears the master. Lets you preview the next track.

D

DAW
Digital Audio Workstation — software for recording, arranging, and mixing (LMMS, Ableton, FL Studio, etc.).
Drop
The moment energy hits after a build or break — often when the full beat returns.

E

Energy curve
How intensity rises and falls across a song or set — the emotional arc.
EQ
Equalizer — a tool that boosts or cuts bass, mids, or highs so sounds sit clearly together.

F

Filter
Cuts highs or lows over time — common DJ FX for builds and transitions.
FOSS
Free and Open Source Software — e.g. Mixxx, LMMS. Free to use and study.
Four-on-the-floor
Kick on every beat (1-2-3-4) — classic house and much of EDM.

G

Gain
Input level of a channel. Set gain so meters sit healthy before you ride the fader.
Ghost note
A quiet drum hit between main hits that adds groove without stealing focus.

H

Headphone cue
PFL (pre-fader listen) button that sends a channel to your headphones so you can preview without putting it on the master / dancefloor.
Headroom
Safety space below clipping so peaks don't distort.
Hi-hat
A bright cymbal sound that fills rhythm between kicks and snares (open or closed).
Highs
High frequencies (above ~4 kHz) — hats, air, sparkle. Easy to make harsh if boosted too much.
Hot cue
A saved cue you can trigger instantly with one button — great for drops and loops.

I

Interface
Audio interface — box that gets clean sound in/out of your computer with better converters than a laptop jack.
Intro
Opening section of a track, often sparse — ideal for mixing in.
Isolator
A mixer EQ style that can fully kill a band (bass, mids, or highs) when turned all the way down — classic club bass-swap tool.

J

Jog wheel
The large platter on a deck used to scratch, scrub, or nudge timing in tiny amounts while beatmatching.

K

Key
The tonal home of a song (e.g. A minor). Mixing in compatible keys sounds smoother.
Kick
The low drum hit that usually lands on every beat in house (four-on-the-floor) or on 1 and 3 in many styles.

L

Loop
Repeating a section (e.g. 8 bars) while you prepare the other deck.

M

Master
The main output the audience (or your speakers) hears.
MIDI
Data that says which notes to play, when, and how hard — not the sound itself. Synths turn MIDI into audio.
Mids
Middle frequencies (~250 Hz–4 kHz) where vocals, snares, and much of the musical body live.
MP3
Compressed audio for sharing and phones. Fine for listening; keep a WAV master too.
Mute / Solo
Mute silences a track; solo isolates it so you can hear it alone while mixing.

N

Nudging
Tiny temporary speed taps (or jog wheel moves) to line up beats without changing the overall BPM.

O

Outro
Ending section — often stripped down so you can mix out cleanly.

P

Pan
Left/right placement of a sound in the stereo field.
Pattern
A short repeating clip of drums or notes — the building block before a full song arrangement.
Pentatonic
A five-note scale that is hard to sound 'wrong' — great for first melodies.
PFL
Pre-Fader Listen — hearing a channel in headphones before the channel fader (and usually before the crossfader) affects the room.
Phrase
A musical sentence made of bars — often 8, 16, or 32 bars. DJs mix at phrase boundaries.
Phrase boundary
The start of a new 8/16/32-bar phrase — preferred place to begin a mix.
Pitch fader
Control that speeds up or slows down a track to match BPM with another track.

Q

Quantize
Snap notes/hits to the grid so timing is clean (you can loosen later with swing).

R

Reference track
A commercial song you A/B against while mixing to check balance and tone.
Root note
The home pitch of a scale or chord — bass often follows roots under the kick.

S

Sample pack
A folder of drum hits, loops, and one-shots. Use free/legal packs only.
Send / return
A shared effects bus (e.g. one reverb) that multiple tracks can feed lightly.
Sidechain
Often: ducking the bass volume briefly when the kick hits so both stay clear (pumping effect).
Snare
A mid/high drum that often hits on beats 2 and 4 (the backbeat).
Swing
Slight timing shift that makes rigid 16th notes feel groovier (often 55–60% on sequencers).
Sync
Software feature that auto-matches tempo (and sometimes phase). Useful while learning; still practice by ear.

T

Trainwreck
A messy mix where beats fight each other — usually tempo or phrase misalignment.
Transition
How you move from one track to the next (EQ swap, filter, cut, phrase blend).
Transport
Play, stop, record controls in a DAW or practice tool.
Trim
Another name for channel gain — the input level before the EQ and fader. Set it so meters sit healthy, not slamming red.

V

VU meter
Level lights that show how loud a channel or the master is. Green is healthy; red means clipping — turn down gain.

W

WAV
Uncompressed audio file format — preferred for high-quality exports and mastering handoff.
